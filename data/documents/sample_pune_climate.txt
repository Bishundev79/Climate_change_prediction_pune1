Pune Climate and Agriculture Overview

Pune, located in the Indian state of Maharashtra, experiences a tropical wet and dry climate bordering on a hot semi-arid climate. The city is situated on the leeward side of the Sahyadri mountain range, which significantly influences its weather patterns.

Historical Events
- 2005 Floods: In July 2005, Pune and surrounding areas experienced unprecedented rainfall. While Mumbai was severely affected, Pune also saw significant waterlogging and disruption. The Mutha river breached its banks in low-lying areas.
- 2015 El Niño Impact: The strong El Niño event of 2015 led to a severe rainfall deficit during the monsoon season. This resulted in delayed sowing of Kharif crops and significant agricultural stress in the rural areas surrounding Pune.
- 1997 Heatwave: A severe heatwave struck Pune in May 1997, pushing maximum temperatures well above 40°C consistently for over a week, causing increased incidences of heatstroke and crop damage.
- Rising Average Temperatures: Over the past four decades, Pune has seen a steady increase in its average summer temperatures, often attributed to rapid urbanization, loss of green cover, and global climate change trends. The "cool city" reputation Pune held in the 1980s has slowly diminished.

Agricultural Context
The agriculture around Pune is heavily dependent on the Southwest Monsoon (June to September). 
- Major Kharif crops include jowar (sorghum), bajra (pearl millet), and soybeans. 
- Rabi crops, which rely on the residual soil moisture post-monsoon, include wheat, gram, and onions.
Cash crops like sugarcane are cultivated in areas with assured irrigation, often drawing water from dams like Khadakwasla, Panshet, Temghar, and Varasgaon. Any deviation in monsoon rainfall directly impacts the reservoir levels of these dams, thereby dictating the agricultural output and drinking water supply for the entire year.
